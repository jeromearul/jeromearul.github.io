◆ Non-Regular Crystal From Pieces v3 ◆

Photograph flat off-cuts of sheet material → fold their triangles into one closed 3D crystal.
STEP 1 — Photo Input
Your photo never leaves this page: it is read straight into browser memory, nothing is stored or uploaded anywhere.
   
Standard: light pieces on a dark background, not touching, shot straight-on, outside surface of each piece facing the camera.
STEP 2 — Piece Detection
Otsu's method picks the brightness cut between pieces and background; flood fill labels each blob; Moore neighbour-tracing walks around its outline.
—
STEP 3 — Triangulation & Cuts
Douglas-Peucker turns each outline into a clean polygon; ear clipping cuts an n-sided piece into triangles with n−3 cuts (the provable minimum); higher complexity splits every triangle into four for more facets.
Complexity: minimal triangles many facets
—
STEP 4 — Edge Pairing
On a closed solid every edge is shared by exactly two triangles, so all triangle edges are paired two-by-two, preferring equal lengths. Each candidate pairing is verified with Euler's polyhedral formula V − E + F = 2 before acceptance; mismatched lengths are averaged (reported as "edge adjustment").
—
STEP 5 — Folding Solver
Position-based dynamics: corners are scattered in space, then repeatedly nudged until every edge reaches its measured length — like letting a wire-frame relax into shape. Several random restarts; the best (largest, most accurate) fold wins.
—
STEP 6 — Hull Closure
The crystal is the convex hull of the folded corners (quickhull algorithm). A convex hull is always closed and watertight with no intersecting or flapping faces — corners that land inside are trimmed off as waste, which is accepted.
